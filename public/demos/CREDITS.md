# Demo imagery credits

Three concepts ship. Marigold Kitchen carries nine licensed stock photographs and
Rowanwood Dental three — twelve photographs in all, each listed below with its
photographer, subject and licence page. BlueLine Plumbing's plates are generated
artwork rather than photographs, so no licence is claimed for them; they are recorded
in their own section first, because their provenance is a different kind.

Every file here is written as a full path (`demos/<concept>/<file>.webp`), because ten
of these file names were also used by concepts that no longer ship: all nine Marigold
plates carry the same names as the retired Ember & Oak files, and `hero-pipes.webp` is
both a retired FlowRight Plumbing name and a live BlueLine Plumbing one. A bare name
therefore cannot be checked against the disk without guessing which concept owns it.
Nothing in this file names a photograph that is not in the
repository: the retired FlowRight Plumbing, Ember & Oak and Maison Lune cards lost
their files in the same pass that retired the concepts, so their credits went with
them. `git log` keeps that history.

All twelve photographs are used under the **Unsplash License**
(https://unsplash.com/license) — "Free to use under the Unsplash License."

## BlueLine Plumbing — /demos/blueline-plumbing

Files in `demos/blueline-plumbing/` (17): a hero, three before/after pairs, a service
van, and nine service plates.

These are **generated images that arrived with the supplied reference design** for
this concept. They are not documentary photographs of a real job, not stock
photographs, and not credited to a photographer — there is no camera behind any of
them, so no licence is claimed and none is needed. The concept says the same thing on
screen, in its own words: the story band carries a one-line provenance note, the
before/after band labels its plates "Illustrative before/after images shown for this
portfolio demonstration", and the footer states that all names, reviews, images and
contact details are illustrative.

Two of the seventeen were inspected frame by frame, and both depict generated people
with visible faces:

- `demos/blueline-plumbing/hero-pipes.webp` — one technician at a valve, face in
  three-quarter view.
- `demos/blueline-plumbing/service-van.webp` — two technicians beside a van, both
  facing the camera, and near-identical to each other in the way generated faces
  usually are.

Nobody in either is a real person, so nothing here can be misattributed to a client or
a worker. It does break the standard the rest of this file holds its photographs to —
"no face is visible" — which is why it is written down rather than quietly kept. The
remaining fifteen files were not individually inspected, so nothing is claimed about
them either way.

## Rowanwood Dental — /demos/rowanwood-dental

### The fourth frame — declined, and no longer on disk

A fourth candidate, five periodontal probes laid in a row on a white tray (orange,
purple and black handles, by Caroline LM,
https://unsplash.com/photos/orange-and-black-audio-jack-hxXO3xKQHSg), is **not** used
and its file was deleted with the retirement pass. It is filed on Unsplash under the
auto-generated title "orange and black audio jack"; it is a set of dental probing
instruments, confirmed by looking at the downloaded frame — the standard applied to
every plate here.

**Ruling 17 (the brand-text guard) — why it never reached the page:** the frame carries
legible **"DEPPFLEX"** manufacturer text, and it is not on the tray packaging but
*molded into all four instrument handles dead-centre*. Measured before deciding, not
assumed from the filename: three candidate crops were exported and each was read back,
and the only crop that clears the text leaves an empty tray — there is no crop that
keeps the instruments and drops the brand. A fictional practice cannot be photographed
standing over somebody else's product, so the concept ships on three honest photos
rather than one brand placard. If a fourth image is ever wanted, the text has to be
removed by retouching, not cropping. `scratch/dental-flow.cjs` asserts that the file
name appears nowhere in the page's markup, image attributes or stylesheets.

### Rowanwood Dental — shots considered and rejected

- The best-matched "interior of an empty modern dental clinic" frames in the search
  results carry the **Unsplash+** lock (`plus.unsplash.com`) or a Getty Images
  attribution and offer no "Download free" — premium stock, not the Unsplash License.
  Rejected on licence.
- `1762625570087-6d98fca29531` (a treatment room with wall art) — free and faceless,
  but the wall text is a legible lyric quoting a named band, and a CCTV dome sits in
  the corner of the ceiling. A fictional practice cannot inherit either. Rejected.

## Notes

- No face is visible in any of the **twelve stock photographs** listed above.
  Marigold's two story plates show hands only —
  `demos/marigold-kitchen/story-hands.webp` is cropped to forearms and a table,
  `demos/marigold-kitchen/story-dessert.webp` to hands and a bowl with a strand of hair
  at the very edge — and its hero and gallery plates contain no people at all, so no
  guest or cook could ever have been recognised there. Rowanwood's three plates contain
  no people at all: two empty rooms and an empty waiting area, so no patient, dentist
  or receptionist is depicted on that concept and nobody is named there either.
- BlueLine's generated plates are the exception to the no-face standard and are handled
  in their own section above.
- A before/after drag slider was **dropped from the stock-photo demos** and never came
  back on one: no genuine licence-verifiable pair of the same space was available, and
  a "before" was not to be faked from a real photograph with a filter, a crop, or a
  desaturated copy. The plumbing concept does ship a working slider, and it can say so
  because nothing is being passed off — both halves of each BlueLine pair are generated
  images of the same fictional job, and the band labels them illustrative on screen.
